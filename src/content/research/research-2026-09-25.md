## AD\-WM targets counterfactual action selection

**This week’s deeper reading.** Publication date: 2026\-09\-24\. Event date: the same date, when this arXiv preprint was published; it is not a peer\-reviewed publication date\. Qiu, Chen, Cao, Shi, Huo, and Gao introduce AD\-WM, a latent world model intended for image\-goal model predictive control \(MPC\)\. The paper’s central argument is that training only on observed transitions can yield low factual prediction error while failing to distinguish alternative actions from the same state—the comparison MPC actually needs\. AD\-WM uses residual latent prediction plus two predictor\-level action\-recovery losses: inverse recovery of an action embedding and a normalized recovery objective motivated by conditional mutual information\. The recovery heads train generated latent transitions but are removed for deployment, so the reported MPC procedure itself is unchanged\. The authors also propose diagnostics focused on CEM’s selected elite candidates, arguing that elite regret better reflects closed\-loop control than either factual prediction error or ranking across an entire candidate bank\.  Author\-reported results are strongest on their Cube hard\-start evaluation: their matched LeWM reproduction has 3\.7% mean hard\-start success, while AD\-WM reports 52\.0%\. Across five simulation environments, AD\-WM reports higher mean success than the matched reproduction in Cube, Reacher, TwoRoom, and Scene, but lower success in PushT\. The paper also reports zero\-shot tests on a Franka setup using a frozen V\-JEPA 2 encoder and matched DROID post\-training: basic pick\-and\-place rises from 19/45 to 32/45 successful trials\. This is a useful contribution because it frames world\-model evaluation around selection among candidate actions rather than prediction accuracy alone, and it supplies ablations separating residual parameterization, inverse recovery, and normalized recovery\.

**Why it matters.** For teams building vision\-based robotic planners, the practical research question is whether a learned dynamics model preserves small action\-dependent changes that matter for selecting controls, not whether it simply forecasts held\-out latent states accurately\. The authors report that their best factual\-MSE model, LeWM, has the lowest hard\-start success, whereas AD\-WM has higher factual error but higher hard\-start success\. Their elite\-regret diagnostic may therefore be useful as an offline planning\-facing metric, although it is validated only in their Cube analysis\. The reported real\-robot result is notable because the comparison holds the encoder, data source, predictor architecture, CEM planner, clipping, and deployment stack fixed while changing transition parameterization and auxiliary objectives\.

**Evidence.** The authors say the auxiliary recovery heads are removed at deployment, leaving the MPC planner unchanged\. ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30264v1)) The abstract reports improved basic pick\-and\-place transfer in the Franka setup\. ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30264v1))

**Limits.** These are author\-reported preprint results, not independent replication\. The robot study uses 82 trials per model, manual grasp/move/place image subgoals, non\-randomized model blocks, one site, one camera, and one backbone; it therefore does not establish broad robot generalization\. Simulation comparisons to external methods use differing checkpoints or inference interfaces, so the controlled evidence is principally the AD\-WM versus reproduced\-LeWM comparison\. Scene’s reported gain remains unresolved by the authors’ paired seed test \(p=0\.13\)\. The paper also says simulation and robot post\-training use different auxiliary weights, and its selection diagnostics assess fixed candidate banks rather than adaptive CEM trajectories\.

**Try this.** Treat AD\-WM as a testable training modification for existing latent\-space, CEM\-based MPC rather than as evidence to replace a planner wholesale\. A sensible reproduction would retain the current planner, add residual prediction and predictor\-level action recovery, then evaluate both task success and the quality of candidates retained by search\. Validate on randomized robot trials and without manually structured subgoal pipelines before relying on the reported transfer result\.

## Agentic context improves Hebrew tweet classification

Published as an arXiv preprint on 2026\-09\-24\. The authors evaluate an agent that can retrieve tweet metadata, author information, prior posts, and retweet\-network context before classifying whether a Hebrew tweet endorses a conspiracy theory\.

**Why it matters.** The paper frames detection as interpretation of intent rather than detection of keywords: a post can quote, satirize, criticize, or endorse the same claim\. In the authors’ manually annotated, adversarial 504\-tweet evaluation set, the full agentic condition reported 0\.790 accuracy and 0\.730 F1, versus 0\.603 accuracy and 0\.535 F1 for the text\-only model\. This suggests selective retrieval can be more useful than attaching all available context to every prompt\.

**Evidence.** The authors report that the full agentic workflow improved F1 from 0\.535 to 0\.730 over the text\-only baseline\. ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30250v1)) The annotated evaluation set contained 504 tweets\. ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30250v1)) The paper explicitly limits its intended deployment\. ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30250v1))

**Limits.** Author\-reported preprint results concern a specific Hebrew Twitter archive and models, with deliberately difficult sampling; the authors did not optimize hyperparameters and say other models may differ\. Context also introduced errors\. They caution that analyzing user history and networks raises privacy concerns and state the system is better suited to research than automated moderation without human review\.

**Try this.** Researchers building social\-media classifiers can test on\-demand retrieval of prior author posts and local network signals, while preserving temporal boundaries and requiring human review for consequential moderation decisions\.

## RAPID turns a visual demonstration into a reusable robot program

Publication date: 2026\-09\-24\. Event: the authors posted the v1 arXiv preprint describing RAPID, a framework that uses a single visual human demonstration and language description to generate, verify, and refine robotic manipulation programs\.

**Why it matters.** RAPID targets a practical bottleneck in robot coding agents: they commonly assume task\-success signals, hand\-built action primitives, and resettable verification environments\. The authors instead reconstruct a simulation environment, infer task predicates and primitives, then use iterative execution and revision\. Its object\-centric relational representation aims to preserve a demonstrated strategy while adapting trajectory optimization to changed scene geometry and physical properties\. This is especially relevant for contact\-rich, nonprehensile manipulation, where reusable fixed primitive libraries are difficult to design\.

**Evidence.** The authors report that RAPID constructs reusable programs from a single visual human demonstration\. ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30249v1)) In the authors' simulation table, full RAPID has a reported average success rate of 0\.759±0\.024 across eight nonprehensile tasks\. ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30249v1)) The authors report a real\-robot deployment using a Franka Research 3 arm and RGB\-D camera\. ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30249v1))

**Limits.** Author\-reported findings from a preprint, not independent validation\. Evaluation covers eight simulated nonprehensile tasks, LIBERO\-Pro simulation, and eight real\-world task types; the source reports 10 real test scenes per task but does not supply numerical real\-world results in the provided text\. The approach also depends on reconstructed RGB\-D scenes, estimated physical properties, and simulation fidelity\.

**Try this.** For robotics teams, the work suggests a route to amortize programming effort: construct and verify a task strategy once from a demonstration, then bind semantic roles in new scenes\. The reported construction cost is tens of minutes per task, while the authors say scene binding takes seconds; suitability in unstructured or deformable\-object settings remains unestablished here\.

## Rolling\-WAM reuses future robot predictions

Published as an arXiv preprint on September 24, 2026\. The authors introduce Rolling\-WAM, a world\-action model that retains partially denoised video\-action predictions across closed\-loop replanning cycles instead of regenerating an entire horizon from noise each time\. The next action chunk is completed for execution while future chunks are progressively refined as new observations arrive\.

**Why it matters.** For robot\-control teams using generative world\-action models, the approach targets a deployment bottleneck: iterative future\-video and action generation can delay control updates\. The paper reports evaluation on LIBERO, RoboTwin 2\.0, and three Unitree G1 humanoid tasks\. In its controlled A100 measurement, the authors report 215 ms steady\-state replanning latency for Rolling\-WAM versus 978 ms for Joint\-WAM; these are author\-reported results, not an independent replication\.

**Evidence.** The authors report that Rolling\-WAM distributes joint denoising across successive replanning cycles\. ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30247v1)) In the reported default latency comparison, Rolling\-WAM used two denoising steps per update and took 215 ms\. ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30247v1))

**Limits.** This is a preprint published September 24, 2026, rather than peer\-reviewed evidence\. The reported latency excludes initialization and warm\-up, uses one NVIDIA A100 and a specified RoboTwin configuration, and may not transfer to other hardware, task dynamics, control frequencies, or rapid scene changes\. The authors also warn that retained predictions can lag changes and misguide actions, especially with long windows\.

**Try this.** Consider it as a design option for closed\-loop manipulation systems where preserving predicted visual futures matters but full joint denoising is too slow\. Reproduce latency and failure behavior on target hardware and safety\-critical tasks before deployment\.

## Coding Agents for Generalized Task and Motion Planning Problems

Publication date: 2026\-09\-24\. In this arXiv preprint, Matteo Merler and collaborators evaluate Claude Code with Opus 5 and Codex with GPT\-5\.6 Sol or GPT\-6 Astra as program\-synthesis agents for generalized task\-and\-motion planning\. The authors report 980 generated programs, each evaluated on 100 held\-out simulated instances, across 28 KinDER and PDDLStream environments\. They freeze the generated policy before testing, so no LLM is used at test time\. Author\-reported results say all three configurations surpassed hand\-engineered planners in mean success on the 16 environments with planners; Astra averaged 95%, Opus 82%, and Sol 56%, versus 47% for planners\. The paper also reports that Astra policies averaged 1\.3 ms per action in a selected subset of perfect runs\. This is an evaluation of simplified, fully observed simulation tasks, not evidence of performance with real robots or perception uncertainty\.

**Why it matters.** The work frames coding agents as a potential alternative to task\-and\-motion\-planning\-specific engineering: agents can probe a simulator, write and revise a reusable policy, then execute that policy without model calls\. The comparison also separates interactive agentic synthesis from one\-shot and feedback\-limited LLM program generation\.

**Evidence.** The authors report that their three coding\-agent configurations exceeded hand\-engineered planners, one\-shot generation, and an LLM generalized\-planning baseline in mean success\. ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30233v1))

**Limits.** Author\-reported finding\. The setup supplies fully observed object\-centric states and simulator access during synthesis; the authors state that it does not establish performance under perception uncertainty\. Because model training data are undisclosed, the authors also cannot exclude earlier exposure to benchmark code\. Results are simulation\-only, use five runs per method and environment, and include source\-code\-access conditions that are not comparable to a black\-box deployment setting\.

**Try this.** For robotics researchers, the practical takeaway is to evaluate coding\-agent policy synthesis as a baseline when a simulator and held\-out procedural task instances exist\. Treat the reported scores as benchmark results rather than deployment evidence; test transfer, sensing, safety constraints, and compute budgets separately before using such policies on hardware\.
